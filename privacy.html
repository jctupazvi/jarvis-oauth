JARVIS Privacy Policy

Review date: 6 November 2026

Final review draft — publication not approved. The disclosures below incorporate the operator’s approved privacy decisions. Targeted operational checks remain unresolved; this document is not a compliance certification. Informational website publication is separate from OAuth production clearance.

JARVIS is exclusively a private personal/household assistant. Only its owner authorizes Google Gmail and Calendar access. It is not distributed, offered commercially or used to obtain third-party Google account authorization. This policy covers the assistant’s Google integrations and this informational website. The website is separate from the assistant and does not offer public registration, Google sign-in or token collection.

JARVIS is privately operated by its owner. For privacy questions or owner-authorized manual deletion requests, contact judithannettupaz@gmail.com.

1. Information accessed and purposes

Google access requires the account holder’s OAuth consent. JARVIS additionally checks the relevant registered-user, channel and tool permissions.

  • Calendar: selected calendar identifiers and names; event titles, dates, times, descriptions, locations, attendees, recurrence and reminder information as needed for authorized scheduling features. JARVIS can read events and, where separately enabled and confirmed, create, modify or delete events on eligible calendars.
  • Reminders: enabled background processing reads selected calendars and maintains local reminder records. Event titles and times may be spoken or sent to a configured WhatsApp destination without a separate confirmation for every scheduled reminder.
  • Gmail: message and thread identifiers, sender and recipient headers, subjects, timestamps, available message bodies and attachment metadata, for authorized mailbox reading, extractive summaries, sending and replies. The current read path inspects attachment names, types, sizes and references; it does not offer general incoming-attachment download or analysis.
  • Outgoing communications: recipients, subject, message text, reply/threading information and metadata for explicitly selected local attachments. Sending and replying require the applicable permissions and confirmation. The current reply flow is sender-only; it does not implement Reply All.
  • Assistant operation: voice input, transcripts, conversation context, user and channel identifiers, permissions, requests, delivery outcomes and operational diagnostics needed to provide and protect the service.

Background Gmail polling and sender-initiated email commands are disabled in the reviewed deployment. Authorized on-demand mailbox requests remain available. Calendar reminders are a separate background feature.

2. Google permissions and credentials

The existing Gmail authorization flow requests gmail.readonly for mailbox access and gmail.send for sending and replies. Calendar authorization requests calendar.readonly and calendar.events.owned. Scope names here omit the common Google authorization prefix for readability. JARVIS does not request Gmail modification or deletion permission through these flows.

OAuth access and refresh tokens allow continued access without collecting your Google password. Credentials and tokens are stored in the operator’s local environment, separate from this public website. Revoking a Google grant and changing JARVIS user permissions are different actions.

3. External processing and recipients

Google processes API requests and the information needed to read or change authorized Google resources. Outgoing email content and selected attachments are delivered to their recipients through Gmail.

Groq: the reviewed deployment uses this external conversational AI provider. Current requests and retained conversation messages are sent to it when an AI response is generated. Although Gmail summaries and Google-tool readouts use deterministic code directly, those readouts can remain in conversation history and be included in a later AI request. Local-model fallback exists; it does not make the whole service local-only.

WhatsApp / Meta and OpenWA: WhatsApp interactions, replies and configured reminders can contain Google-derived information. OpenWA connects the assistant to WhatsApp. Messages are processed by the messaging service and delivered to the relevant participants or authorized group. The operator cannot erase copies already received by recipients merely by deleting local records.

Tavily: optional web retrieval sends search-query text to this external search provider. Sensitive text included in a query can therefore leave the local environment. This policy does not promise a technical prohibition on all Google-derived text entering user requests or later conversation context.

The operator authorizes external processing only as necessary for the disclosed, authorized features. That operational decision does not establish that every provider term or downstream use satisfies Google’s requirements. Provider retention, secondary-use, human-access and training controls must be compatible with those requirements; owner-only use does not waive them. This policy does not promise zero retention or local-only processing.

Groq’s public documentation describes inference-retention exceptions and configurable data controls; the operator’s account-specific settings and governing service agreement have not been independently verified. Tavily’s public terms include AI-functionality clauses allowing training-related and downstream uses. Whether those clauses cover JARVIS’s basic-search usage or are overridden by an applicable agreement remains unresolved. These require operational clarification before those Google-data processing paths can be cleared as policy-compatible. The review did not establish that prohibited training has actually occurred.

4. Storage and retention

Local persistent records include authorization and configuration data, Gmail message metadata and synchronization state, email-send envelopes and outcomes, reply references, calendar selections, and reminder titles, times and delivery state. Send envelopes can retain recipients, subjects and message text; they are not limited to anonymous audit counters.

Conversation text is held in application memory with configurable bounds and inactivity expiry. In the reviewed deployment the limit is ten exchanges and the inactivity timeout is thirty minutes. Session expiry or deletion does not remove independent email, reminder, authorization or audit records. Raw voice recordings are not deliberately archived by the reviewed speech path.

The reviewed code does not provide one general automatic deletion deadline for persistent Google-derived records, credentials or operational logs. They can remain until operator maintenance or deletion. Temporary outgoing-file snapshots have expiry and cleanup mechanisms; that is not a retention guarantee for all data.

Optional detailed web diagnostics are disabled in the reviewed deployment. If separately authorized, bounded records may contain query text, source excerpts, model evidence and output. Credential-pattern redaction does not guarantee removal of all personal information. The recorder stops at its size limit and has no automatic age-based deletion.

Retention is owner-controlled, with no automatic universal expiry. Recovery practices include retaining application versions and selected operational recovery records; this does not establish a scheduled backup service or comprehensive backup of Google data. No backup schedule, encryption guarantee, complete restore capability or comprehensive backup deletion is promised.

5. Access and security

JARVIS uses registered-user and tool-specific permission checks, separate calendar selections, confirmation for consequential Google writes, and controls intended to reduce duplicate sends and reminder deliveries. The local interface depends on a trusted operator-controlled environment. These controls are not a claim that every local interface independently authenticates every person with access to the host.

Google API and configured cloud-model requests use HTTPS. Routine provider telemetry is designed to omit message bodies and token values, but the application also retains content-bearing operational records described above. Application-level encryption of all local data has not been established. Host, storage and backup protections require operator verification. No security certification, independently assessed compliance status or absolute security guarantee is claimed.

6. Google API data and Limited Use requirements

The stated owner-only use falls within the Google personal-use verification exception. This exempts OAuth verification, not user-data obligations. In production status does not mean Google has verified or approved the application.

Compliance status: A website statement and operator approval alone do not establish compliance. OAuth production clearance remains unresolved pending targeted processing, consent and security checks. Publication of an accurate informational policy does not resolve those checks.

The Google API Services User Data Policy and applicable Google Workspace user data and developer policy govern use of information obtained through Google APIs, including derived information. The completed review does not establish that all enabled JARVIS processing meets these requirements.

These requirements limit Google-derived data to appropriate disclosed user-facing purposes and permitted transfers, restrict human access, and prohibit sale, advertising, credit-related uses and training or improvement of general-purpose AI models beyond the permitted personalized use. JARVIS has no identified application training, advertising or data-sale workflow in the reviewed implementation. That observation does not establish the practices of external providers or rule out prohibited downstream secondary use.

Necessary transfers for user-facing features can be permissible with appropriate consent and policy-compatible handling. Broad processor permissions or disclosure alone do not make an incompatible transfer permissible. JARVIS is not represented as approved, certified or verified by Google.

7. Disconnecting Google and requesting deletion

You can review and revoke JARVIS’s grants in Google Account third-party connections. Revocation prevents continued access under the revoked grant once effective; it does not itself delete stored JARVIS data, Google events or email, recipient copies, or cached reminders. Locally retained reminder information may still be processed until the operator disables the relevant service or removes that data.

JARVIS has no single verified in-app action that disconnects every Google integration and purges all related records and backups. Email judithannettupaz@gmail.com to request an owner-authorized manual review and deletion within available capabilities. The owner reviews the request and authority before authorizing manual action. This does not promise removal of all persistent records, recipient copies or provider-held data, comprehensive backup deletion, immediate erasure or a fixed completion deadline. Deleting a conversation session removes that in-memory session, not independent operational records.

8. This informational website

These pages contain no application-added analytics scripts, advertising scripts, embedded trackers or Google authorization form. If hosted on GitHub Pages, GitHub records visitor IP addresses for security purposes and handles hosting-related data under its privacy statement. Visiting this website does not transfer your Gmail mailbox or Calendar to the public repository.

9. Changes and contact

Material changes to Google-data use or sharing require updated disclosures and consent where applicable before the new use begins. For privacy questions and owner-authorized manual deletion requests, contact judithannettupaz@gmail.com. Do not send passwords, OAuth tokens or authorization codes with a request.
